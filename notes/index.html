How it’s made

Notes

VIPER 11 is a free flight simulator: a fighter jet you fly from a real runway, up through the clouds, in a browser tab. Each of these short notes explains one reason it looks, sounds or feels the way it does.

  • Note 1Clouds you can fly throughHow a browser draws a cumulus you can climb into, one step of a ray at a time.
  • Note 2The GoPro that doesn’t existWhy VIPER 11 looks like footage and not like a game: a lens, a canopy and a grade that were never real.
  • Note 3The loudest thing is silenceA fighter cockpit is quiet. Building the sound of one meant taking almost everything out.
  • Note 4Why your vision tunnelsWhat nine times your own weight does to your eyes, and the small model that brings it to the screen.
  • Note 5A real airfieldReal elevation, real aerial photographs and a painted runway, streamed around the jet as it flies.
  • Note 6Fly-by-wire in a browserA six-degree-of-freedom F-16 with its control laws, simplified honestly and tuned to feel right.
  • Note 7Flying with a real stickFlight sticks, throttles and pedals through the Gamepad API, and why the mouse now turns your head.
  • Note 8Jets made of numbersHow the F-35C and the Super Hornet were built in code from fact sheets and locked reference images, and why the F-16 was rebuilt alongside them.
  • Note 9Less yellow, more heatA real afterburner by day is a deep-orange glow inside the nozzle and a shimmer in the air behind it. Getting there meant taking the yellow out.
  • Note 10The glory around your shadowOver a cloud deck with the sun behind you, the jet’s shadow wears a ring of colour. Where it comes from, and how the game draws it.
  • Note 11Finding the runwayHill and Key West join Nellis, and the small things that bring you from anywhere in the sky to the right end of the right runway.
  • Note 12Two-one left, cleared for takeoffThe controllers who talk you from the parking spot to the runway and home again, and why their numbers now come in whole sentences.
  • Note 13The throttle pushes backPlayStation and Xbox pads in a browser: their own glyphs, rumble from the flight model, and a DualSense trigger you squeeze through MIL.
  • Note 14Out of a hundredHow the game grades a landing, sets the same daily challenge for everyone, and checks a score it can’t trust.
  • Note 15The bobblehead on the dashA spring that feels every g the jet pulls, six stock passengers, and a workshop that makes your own while you watch.
